# Basic Rules

## Roleplaying

This is, at its core, the adult version of "pretend". Step into your character's life and pretend like you are them. Have fun. Act the way the character would. Be creative. Act it out!

There will be a DM (Dungeon Master) who tells the story. They will have to change things based on how the characters respond. This is a difficult role and requires quick thinking and flexibility. You can have a great story planned out, and the characters might choose to not follow the obvious path. You'll need to either find a way to get them back on track, or go with the flow and figure out what happens based on what they do.

The other players will take on various class roles with either the pre-defined characters here, or they can make up their own if they would like to.

## Alignment

Each character has an alignment consisting of two categories: "ethical" and "moral". The premade characters will skew more towards lawful and good, however custom characters can go the other direction as long as they have a reason to follow the story. Enemies will skew the other direction.

### Ethical

How much do you care about the rules?

- Lawful - Must follow the laws of the land / religion
- Neutral - Doesn't try to follow or disobey, just fit in
- Chaotic - Does whatever they want in a selfish manner

### Moral

Do you really wish harm upon everyone?

- Good - Wants what is best for society and the world
- Neutral - Wants what is best for themselves
- Evil - Wants to hurt others

## Combat and Skill Checks

If the group gets into a battle, it will rely on some simplified combat mechanics. Combat is turn-based. Speed determines who goes first, both within the party, as well as between the party and the enemies. Each character will have a set of possible abilities they can use. These abilities will rely on skill checks. In general, the player will roll a d20 to determine if the ability succeeds, and if it does, potentially roll other dice to deal damage, and add modifiers. For attacks, the ability will succeed as long as it is not a critical miss (explained below). For other abilities, the dice requirements will be listed with the character's move. Success thresholds will be listed in the story. If thresholds are not listed, the DM can make it up (ex. 8+ for something easy, 12+ for something medium difficulty, 16+ for something hard).

If a d20 roll is involved, there can be either a critical hit or a critical miss. A roll of 20 on the d20 (not including modifiers) is a critical hit. Any damage dealt will be doubled. Any skill used will be used very effectively. For example, if you're trying to gather information, you might find out everything you hoped to get! Conversely, a roll of 1 on the d20 (not including modifiers) is a critical miss. No damage will be dealt to the target, and the character may hurt themself instead. Any skill used will not succeed, and may be harmful. For example, if you're trying to gather information, you might be told a blatant lie and believe it. Either the DM can come up with amusing "successes" or "failures", or the character can be asked to explain what happened. For example, "I was so caught up in the moment that I didn't hold on tightly to my axe, which caused it to fall out of my hands and drop onto my feet. Ouch!"

Movement is often a separate mechanic, but to simplify things for these beginner stories, if you want to change your position (ex. get behind an enemy), you may do so before performing a skill.

If a player's health reaches 0, they are knocked out and will need to be healed before continuing to participate. Let's leave out death as it's a touchy subject, and not fun for a newcomer.

## Outside of Combat

Players will often have to find out information or figure out where to go. There are some skills they will have that will aid in this. Interacting with NPCs (Non-Player Characters), who are roleplayed by the DM, will help them determine the best course of action. The DM can judge how well the players are asking for information and give answers based on that. Sometimes there can be generic skill checks involved, like if you need to trick someone into giving out extra information that you're convinced they have.

There are also times when players will need to notice something important by looking around or paying attention. These are often skill checks, and bad things can happen if no one in the party notices!

Outside of combat, healers can use their healing abilities to prepare the party for the next encounter. Usually there are a limited number of uses for spells like healing, but to simplify things and to make the experience more enjoyable, the party can be fully healed between encounters, assuming you have at least one healer (you should).

## Simplified Modifiers

There are a lot of traits and points to assign and modifiers to be calculated in D&D. Let's simplify things and provide simple integer modifiers per player. These can be changed as characters level up, but these oneshots do not include level up opportunities within the story. If a group wants to do multiple oneshots, you can level the characters up between stories. I would recommend giving the players a choice of 2 or 3 stats to increase by 1.

- Strength
- Speed (Dexterity)
- Smarts (Intelligence/Wisdom)
- Charisma
- Endurance

## Party Balance

Try to ensure that your party has a good selection of character classes. For example, you wouldn't want to have everyone be a warrior, as the group won't be able to heal effectively or handle more delicate situations requiring charisma or speed.

A balanced example would be to have one damage-heavy character, a healer, a spell caster, and one other of any class.

The enemies will explain how to adjust values based on the number of players you have (scaling).

## Have Fun

There are no hard rules here, just guidelines and suggestions. Make things up as you go, and have fun!
